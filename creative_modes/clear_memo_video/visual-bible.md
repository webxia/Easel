# Visual Bible

The visual world is restrained, human-scale and daily: desks, commutes, screens, notebooks, windows, kitchens, clocks, rooms, pauses and small gestures. A picture must advance the observation; it must not merely decorate narration.

- Prefer supplied or approved real evidence for real claims: screenshots, recording excerpts, notes, data or personal material. Never fabricate a screenshot or pass an AI illustration off as evidence.
- When no real material is needed, use low-saturation illustration or quiet photographic B-roll with generous negative space. Avoid glossy AI spectacle, generic cyberpunk, floating dashboards and motivational-poster compositions.
- An illustration may use a recurring symbolic narrator rather than a synthetic portrait. Keep it visually consistent only after a reviewed reference asset exists; otherwise use scene-led imagery and do not pretend there is a locked character.
- Use motion sparingly: slow camera drift, gentle Ken Burns, a short purposeful transition, or one dynamic insert at an emotional turn. Do not animate every still simply to prove that it is video.
- Text belongs in the subtitle and deliberate cover system, not inside generated imagery.
